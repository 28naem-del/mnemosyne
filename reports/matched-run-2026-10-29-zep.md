Zep Cloud scored inside the repository's own matched-benchmark loop, against the same chunk set,
the same questions, the same K and the same packing budget as Mnemosyne and Mem0 OSS. Every table
below was generated by [`scripts/gen-report.mjs`](matched-run-2026-10-29-zep/scripts/gen-report.mjs)
from the merged raw reports in [`matched-run-2026-10-29-zep/`](matched-run-2026-10-29-zep/README.md);
none was typed by hand. `node reports/matched-run-2026-10-29-zep/verify.mjs` recomputes every value
from the per-question records.

## Disclosure and limitations (publish with every Zep number)

- **Who ran it.** This benchmark was designed and run by the Mnemosyne maintainers using the repository's own matched-benchmark harness. We have an interest in the outcome. The harness, adapters, dataset hashes and compressed raw outputs (SHA-256 below) are published so anyone can reproduce or challenge the numbers.
- **Zep tier and version.** Zep Cloud (hosted) via `zep-cloud@3.28.0`, on Zep's free plan (10,000 credits/month). Total cost to the authors: $0. No paid tier was tested. We have no evidence that paid tiers retrieve differently, but we did not verify it.
- **Zep configuration.** Retrieval used `graph.search` with `scope=episodes`, and results were ranked in the order Zep returned them. Zep's fact and edge retrieval (its knowledge-graph output) was not evaluated. Extraction ran vendor-side (Graphiti) with Zep's defaults, and we did not tune it. A diagnostic that re-sorted by Zep's own score roughly doubled Zep's MRR while leaving recall unchanged (section "Diagnostic"). The results describe this configuration, not Zep's best possible setup.
- **Mem0 configuration.** Mem0 OSS ran with LLM extraction off (`infer:false`) and an injected local embedder, matched to Mnemosyne's chunks. That is also not Mem0's default product configuration.
- **What is measured.** Retrieval recall and MRR at K = 5, 10 and 20 against LoCoMo evidence labels. This is not end-to-end answer accuracy, latency under load, or cost per query.
- **Coverage.** LoCoMo only. LongMemEval-S was not run, because at Zep's overage pricing it would have needed about 82,800 credits (about $207), more than this run's budget. Coverage is 10 of 10 LoCoMo conversations (1,986 questions, 1,531 with evidence labels).
- **Point in time.** Run on 29 October 2026. Hosted services change without notice, so results may not hold for later versions.
- **Data handling.** Only public benchmark data was sent to Zep, and the Zep users created for the run are deleted afterwards.
- **Corrections.** If the Zep team considers this configuration unrepresentative, we will rerun it with their recommended settings and publish both results.

## Every loss, plainly

Zep against the field:
- **Zep loses to Mnemosyne hybrid + rerank at every K, on recall and MRR**: recall by 25.28 / 20.74 /
  16.52 points (@5/@10/@20) and MRR by 0.41–0.50.
- **Zep loses to Mem0 OSS (extraction off) at every K**: recall by 13.14 / 10.89 / 10.73 points and
  MRR by 0.26–0.35.
- **Zep loses to Mnemosyne hybrid without rerank at every K**: recall by 12.73 / 10.15 / 9.99 points.
- **Zep beats Mnemosyne's zero-model BM25 on recall@10 (+0.37) and recall@20 (+3.21)**. It loses to
  BM25 at recall@5 (−3.00) and on MRR at every K.
- Zep has the slowest p95 at K = 10 (413 ms, network included). Mem0's p95 is slower than Zep's at
  K = 5 (512 against 409 ms) and K = 20 (540 against 413 ms).

Mnemosyne's own losses in this run (not involving Zep, printed anyway):
- **Mem0 OSS beats our unreranked hybrid at every K** on recall (55.09 / 63.72 / 71.91 against
  54.68 / 62.99 / 71.18), on MRR (0.4633 / 0.4756 / 0.4798 against 0.4478 / 0.4606 / 0.4667), on
  anyHit (61.99 / 70.93 / 79.03 against 60.68 / 69.82 / 78.58) and on allHit (49.84 / 57.54 / 65.25
  against 49.71 / 57.15 / 64.73).
- Only the reranked path beats Mem0, and it does so at every K. In this run its p95 was also below
  Mem0's at every K (354 / 342 / 350 against 512 / 359 / 540 ms); in the 14 September run it was
  slower than Mem0 at K = 10, so do not read the latency ordering as stable.

## Diagnostic: score-sorted Zep results

Question tested: *is Zep's low MRR a retrieval problem, or an ordering artefact?* Zep's `graph.search`
(scope=episodes) attaches a per-episode `score` but returns episodes in roughly creation order. The
repository adapter ranks them in response order. [`scripts/diag.sh`](matched-run-2026-10-29-zep/scripts/diag.sh)
re-ran conversations 0, 1 and 8 (383 labelled questions) with `ZEP_SORT_BY_SCORE=1`, which re-sorts
each response by Zep's own score (descending) before the adapter ranks it. Everything else was
identical. It was search-only, 0 credits, and the change is marked `DIAGNOSTIC` in the mode string.
It is not the adapter's behaviour, and none of its numbers are in the tables above. Raw outputs:
[`diagnostic-scoresorted/`](matched-run-2026-10-29-zep/README.md).

| K | Zep recall, as shipped | Zep recall, score-sorted | Zep MRR, as shipped | Zep MRR, score-sorted |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 43.60 | 44.38 | 0.1967 | 0.3408 |
| 10 | 54.22 | 54.35 | 0.1734 | 0.3555 |
| 20 | 62.76 | 62.89 | 0.1390 | 0.3564 |

Pooled over the 383 labelled questions of conversations 0, 1 and 8, recomputed from the raw part
attempts. Sorting by Zep's own score roughly **doubles Zep's MRR** (0.17 → 0.36 at K = 10). Recall
barely moves (+0.1 to +0.8 points), because the set inside the top K is nearly the same and the
diagnostic is a separate round of hosted search calls. So **Zep's MRR in the headline understates
what its own scores would give; its recall does not**. Even score-sorted, Zep stays below Mem0
(0.4441 MRR@10 on conversation 0) and well below hybrid + rerank on both metrics. Whether the
adapter should sort by score is a repository decision; this run did not change the adapter.

## Wall time, credits and cost

- Phase 1 (`graph.add` of all 5,882 episodes into 10 users) started 10:56:06 UTC. Two early attempts
  hit HTTP 429; it then ran paced at 200 requests per minute.
- Zep's server-side processing ran at about 8–24 episodes/min in aggregate, and at about 0.6–1
  episode/min for conversation 9. This was the long pole.
- Benches ran one conversation at a time once Zep had processed it, so latency was not measured under
  self-contention. The last one finished at 19:56:44 UTC: about **9 h 01 min** end to end. Bench time
  across K = 5 / 10 / 20 was 1,669 + 1,656 + 1,684 s, about 83 min.
- Credits by Zep's 1 credit per 350 bytes rule: **5,926** for 5,882 unique episodes (767,852 bytes),
  spent across three phase-1 invocations (302, 1,007 and 4,617). Every resume after that replayed the
  episode ledger and added nothing.
- Searches: 5,958 `graph.search` calls in the scored benches (1,986 questions × 3 K), plus 1,500 in
  the diagnostic. Under the 350-byte rule searches are not billed; we did not verify that beyond the
  rule.
- Free plan (10,000 credits/month, no billing method): **cost $0**.
